The Story

Zoology was added in 1930, the same year as Wood Turning and Landscape Gardening. It sent Scouts outdoors to study wild animal life, but left out birds, snakes and insects, which had merit badges of their own.

The badge was most popular just before World War II, when 3,599 were earned in 1939. It was discontinued in 1972, after Scouts had earned 64,994 Zoology merit badges.

1942 requirements

  1. Make five field trips of at least two hours each, if possible in different seasons, to watch wild animals. Keep a record of every animal seen, with the date, place, kind of habitat and what it was doing.
  2. Use a classification key to identify at least five animals from different phyla.
  3. Learn the life processes all animals share, such as birth, self-protection, feeding and breathing, and keep a record from your own observation of the life cycle and habits of two animals from different phyla.
  4. On a map of your area, mark the animals found in each habitat, such as forests, streams and marshes, and learn what your state does to protect wild animals.
  5. For at least three months, do one of these: raise a young animal such as a squirrel, rabbit or guinea pig and keep a weekly diary of its habits, food, weight and health; keep an aquarium or "toad pen" and follow frogs, toads, turtles or fish from the egg on; or photograph or sketch microscopic animal life you find and prepare three microscope slides.

Source: Handbook for Boys (1940), requirements in effect January 1, 1942, summarized

Timeline

1930 The Zoology merit badge is introduced. The first Zoology merit badges are earned: 217 that year.

1931 The earliest known Zoology pamphlet printing (Type 3B-1).

1935 Best ranking: 41st of 101 merit badges.

1939 All-time high: 3,599 Zoology merit badges earned.

1942 New requirements take effect January 1, with a choice of three long-term projects.

1972 Last year the Zoology merit badge is offered; it is replaced by General Science Mammals.

What do the patch types mean?
Type A
Square (1911 - 1933). Embroidered on tan cloth and cut into squares. The Scout folded the edges under when sewing it on.
Type B
Wide border crimped (1934 - 1935). The maker folded the edge under and pressed it round, leaving a wide cloth border. Made on depression cloth and heavyweight cloth.
Type C
Tan narrow border crimped (1936 - 1942). The same crimped edge with a narrow border of about 1/8 inch, on tan cloth.
Type D
Fine twill (1942 - 1946). Wartime badges on lighter, finer-weave cloth (fine twill and war cloth) while heavier cloth went to the war effort.
Type E
Khaki narrow border crimped (1947 - 1960). Back to heavier cloth after the war, now in khaki.
Type F
Rolled edge twill (1961 - 1968). The embroidered border is rolled over the edge instead of crimped, with a gauze backing.
Type G
Cloth back (1961 - 1971). Fully embroidered with a cloth back. By about 1969 every merit badge was made this way.
Type H
Plastic back (1972 - 2002). A plastic stiffener on the back. Collectors tell them apart by the back, which can be clear, white, or a very light blue plastic, with waffle-textured and smooth molded versions. These are sorted into variations H1 through H5.

Dates are approximate and can vary by badge.
