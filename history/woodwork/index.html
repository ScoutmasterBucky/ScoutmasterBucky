The Story

Woodwork grew out of Craftsmanship, one of the original 1911 merit badges. In 1927 Craftsmanship was split into separate craft badges, Basketry, Bookbinding, Cement Work, Leathercraft, Metalwork, Pottery, Wood Carving, and Woodwork (then written "Wood Work"), each with its own design.

The earliest pamphlets came from that Craftsmanship family. Printings from 1920 to 1927 carry the title "Craftsmanship – Wood." The badge that followed was Wood Work, and its symbol was a small stool.

In 1952 three badges became one. The January 1952 pamphlet cover reads "Woodwork, replacing Wood Work, Carpentry and Wood Turning merit badges," and it shows a woodworking plane, the symbol the original 1911 Carpentry badge had used. The stool stayed on the patches through Type E. Type F (1961-1968) shows the plane, and since 1969 the badge has shown a group of woodworking tools.

Carpentry came back for one year only in 2010, as one of four historic badges for Scouting's 100th anniversary.

Woodwork was at its most popular during World War II, peaking at 21,589 badges in 1945. By the end of 2020 Scouts had earned 999,863 Woodwork badges, just 137 short of one million.

1940 Wood Work requirements

  1. Design and build a small piece of furniture that uses mortise-and-tenon or dowel joints, such as a plant stand, small table, chair, footstool or writing desk.
  2. Draw plans, or a careful rough sketch, of the piece you chose.

Source: Handbook for Boys (1940), summarized

Timeline

1911 Craftsmanship and Carpentry are among the 57 original merit badges.

1920 The first "Craftsmanship – Wood" pamphlet (Type 2) is printed.

1927 Craftsmanship is split into eight craft badges and Woodwork is born.

1928 First year of Woodwork counts: 12,680 earned.

1930 A separate Wood Turning merit badge is added.

1945 All-time high: 21,589 Woodwork merit badges earned.

1952 A new Woodwork badge replaces Wood Work, Carpentry and Wood Turning, and takes Carpentry's plane as its symbol.

1969 The badge is redesigned with a group of woodworking tools in place of the plane.

2010 Carpentry returns for one year as a historic badge for Scouting's 100th anniversary.

2020 Pandemic year: 3,066 earned, down 32%.

2024 Type 11A cover, the first Woodwork pamphlet with a Scouting America number.

What do the patch types mean?
Type A
Square (1911 - 1933). Embroidered on tan cloth and cut into squares. The Scout folded the edges under when sewing it on.
Type B
Wide border crimped (1934 - 1935). The maker folded the edge under and pressed it round, leaving a wide cloth border. Made on depression cloth and heavyweight cloth.
Type C
Tan narrow border crimped (1936 - 1942). The same crimped edge with a narrow border of about 1/8 inch, on tan cloth.
Type D
Fine twill (1942 - 1946). Wartime badges on lighter, finer-weave cloth (fine twill and war cloth) while heavier cloth went to the war effort.
Type E
Khaki narrow border crimped (1947 - 1960). Back to heavier cloth after the war, now in khaki.
Type F
Rolled edge twill (1961 - 1968). The embroidered border is rolled over the edge instead of crimped, with a gauze backing.
Type G
Cloth back (1961 - 1971). Fully embroidered with a cloth back. By about 1969 every merit badge was made this way.
Type H
Plastic back (1972 - 2002). A plastic stiffener on the back. Collectors tell them apart by the back, which can be clear, white, or a very light blue plastic, with waffle-textured and smooth molded versions. These are sorted into variations H1 through H5.
Type I
Computer designed (1993 - 1995). Flat-edged badges with machine (computer) embroidery and a wider border. Only some badges were made this way.
Type J
Scout Stuff back (2002 - 2009). The "Scout Stuff" Supply Division logo printed on the back.
Type K
BSA 100th anniversary back (2010 - 2014). The BSA 2010 anniversary logo printed on the back.
Type L
Since 1910 back (2015 - 2025). The "Since 1910" logo printed on the back, in large and small logo versions.
Type M
Scouting America back (2025 - current). The Scouting America logo printed on the back. Wildland Fire Management and Competitive Gaming, both new in 2026, start with Type M.

Dates are approximate and can vary by badge.
