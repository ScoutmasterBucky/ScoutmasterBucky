The Story

In 1952 the Citizenship merit badge was broken into 4 individual merit badges; Citizenship in the Home, Citizenship in the Community, Citizenship in the Nation, and World Brotherhood, which in 1972 would eventually turn into Citizenship in the World. These 4 merit badges still utilized the Citizenship merit badge pamphlet until 1972 when they were reorganized into their own subject matters. The World Brotherhood is one of the only merit badges that never had its own merit badge pamphlet book exclusively. The World Brotherhood merit badge was redesigned in 1969 to show the Scout sign on the badge, until it was morphed into the Citizenship in the World merit badge in 1972.

Trivia

Timeline

1952 The World Brotherhood merit badge is introduced. The first World Brotherhood merit badges are earned: 1,305 that year.

1954 Best ranking: 36th of 103 merit badges.

1963 All-time high: 11,523 World Brotherhood merit badges earned.

1969 The badge is redesigned with the Scout sign in front of a blue globe.

1972 Last year the World Brotherhood merit badge is offered; it is replaced by Citizenship in the World.

What do the patch types mean?
Type E
Khaki narrow border crimped (1947 - 1960). Back to heavier cloth after the war, now in khaki.
Type F
Rolled edge twill (1961 - 1968). The embroidered border is rolled over the edge instead of crimped, with a gauze backing.
Type G
Cloth back (1961 - 1971). Fully embroidered with a cloth back. By about 1969 every merit badge was made this way.
Type H
Plastic back (1972 - 2002). A plastic stiffener on the back. Collectors tell them apart by the back, which can be clear, white, or a very light blue plastic, with waffle-textured and smooth molded versions. These are sorted into variations H1 through H5.

Dates are approximate and can vary by badge.
