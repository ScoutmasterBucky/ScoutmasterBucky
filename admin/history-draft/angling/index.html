The Story

The Angling merit badge, the predecessor to today's Fishing merit badge, was a slow gainer in the number earned in its first few years, with less than a dozen being earned in its first 6+ years.

1911 requirements

  1. Catch and name ten different species of fish: salmon or trout to be taken with flies; bass, pickerel, or pike to be caught with rod or reel, muskallonge to be caught by trolling.
  2. Make a bait rod of three joints, straight and sound, 14 oz. or less in weight, 10 feet or less in length, to stand a strain of 1-1/2 lbs. at the tip, 13 lbs. at the grip.
  3. Make a jointed fly-rod 8-10 feet long, 4-8 ozs. in weight, capable of casting a fly sixty feet.
  4. Name and describe twenty-five different species of fish found in North American waters and give a complete list of the fishes ascertained by himself to inhabit a given body of water.
  5. Give the history of the young of any species of wild fish from the time of hatching until the adult stage is reached.

Source: The Official Handbook for Boys (1911)

Timeline

1911 The Angling merit badge is introduced. It is one of the original 57 merit badges.

1913 The first Angling merit badge is earned.

1920 The earliest known Angling pamphlet printing (Type 1C).

1951 All-time high: 4,013 Angling merit badges earned. Best ranking: 48th of 111 merit badges. Last year the Angling merit badge is offered; it is replaced by Fishing.

What do the patch types mean?
Type A
Square (1911 - 1933). Embroidered on tan cloth and cut into squares. The Scout folded the edges under when sewing it on.
Type B
Wide border crimped (1934 - 1935). The maker folded the edge under and pressed it round, leaving a wide cloth border. Made on depression cloth and heavyweight cloth.
Type C
Tan narrow border crimped (1936 - 1942). The same crimped edge with a narrow border of about 1/8 inch, on tan cloth.
Type D
Fine twill (1942 - 1946). Wartime badges on lighter, finer-weave cloth (fine twill and war cloth) while heavier cloth went to the war effort.
Type E
Khaki narrow border crimped (1947 - 1960). Back to heavier cloth after the war, now in khaki.

Dates are approximate and can vary by badge.
