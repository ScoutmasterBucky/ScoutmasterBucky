1911 requirements

  1. Know the proper way to drive, set and clinch a nail.
  2. Know the different kinds of chisels, planes and saws, and how to sharpen and use them.
  3. Know the use of the rule, square, level, plumb-line and mitre.
  4. Know how to use compasses for scribing both regular and irregular lines.
  5. Make an article of furniture with three different standard joints or splices, with at least one surface of highly polished hard or decorative wood. All work to be done without assistance.

Source: The Official Handbook for Boys (1911)

Timeline

1911 The Carpentry merit badge is introduced. It is one of the original 57 merit badges.

1912 The first Carpentry merit badges are earned: 25 that year.

1920 The earliest known Carpentry pamphlet printing (Type 1C).

1928 Best ranking: 4th of 88 merit badges.

1944 All-time high: 23,070 Carpentry merit badges earned.

1952 Last year the Carpentry merit badge is offered; it is replaced by Woodwork.

What do the patch types mean?
Type A
Square (1911 - 1933). Embroidered on tan cloth and cut into squares. The Scout folded the edges under when sewing it on.
Type B
Wide border crimped (1934 - 1935). The maker folded the edge under and pressed it round, leaving a wide cloth border. Made on depression cloth and heavyweight cloth.
Type C
Tan narrow border crimped (1936 - 1942). The same crimped edge with a narrow border of about 1/8 inch, on tan cloth.
Type D
Fine twill (1942 - 1946). Wartime badges on lighter, finer-weave cloth (fine twill and war cloth) while heavier cloth went to the war effort.
Type E
Khaki narrow border crimped (1947 - 1960). Back to heavier cloth after the war, now in khaki.
Type K
BSA 100th anniversary back (2010 - 2014). The BSA 2010 anniversary logo printed on the back.

Dates are approximate and can vary by badge.
