1911 requirements

  1. Be able to paint a door.
  2. Whitewash a ceiling.
  3. Repair gas fittings, sash lines, window and door fastenings.
  4. Replace gas mantles, washers, and electric light bulbs.
  5. Solder.
  6. Hang pictures and curtains.
  7. Repair blinds.
  8. Fix curtains, portiere rods, blind fixtures.
  9. Lay carpets and mend clothing and upholstery.
  10. Repair furniture and china.
  11. Sharpen knives.
  12. Repair gates.
  13. Fix screens on windows and doors.

Source: The Official Handbook for Boys (1911)

Timeline

1911 The Handicraft merit badge is introduced. It is one of the original 57 merit badges. The first Handicraft merit badge is earned.

1920 The earliest known Handicraft pamphlet printing (Type 1C).

1937 Best ranking: 1st of 102 merit badges.

1942 Last year the Handicraft merit badge is offered; it is replaced by Home Repairs.

1944 All-time high: 45,697 Handicraft merit badges earned.

What do the patch types mean?
Type A
Square (1911 - 1933). Embroidered on tan cloth and cut into squares. The Scout folded the edges under when sewing it on.
Type B
Wide border crimped (1934 - 1935). The maker folded the edge under and pressed it round, leaving a wide cloth border. Made on depression cloth and heavyweight cloth.
Type C
Tan narrow border crimped (1936 - 1942). The same crimped edge with a narrow border of about 1/8 inch, on tan cloth.
Type D
Fine twill (1942 - 1946). Wartime badges on lighter, finer-weave cloth (fine twill and war cloth) while heavier cloth went to the war effort.

Dates are approximate and can vary by badge.
