The Story

Wood Turning was added in 1930, the same year as Zoology and Landscape Gardening. It taught Scouts to shape wood on a lathe with gouges and chisels, and its badge showed a lathe.

The badge was never more popular than at the end, when 4,870 were earned in 1951. In 1952 a new Woodwork badge replaced Wood Work, Carpentry and Wood Turning, after Scouts had earned 78,061 Wood Turning merit badges.

1940 requirements

  1. Show how to use the roughing gouge, turning gouge, skew chisel and parting tool, and how to sharpen each one.
  2. Show how to put wood into the lathe and take it out, both between the live and dead centers and on a faceplate.
  3. Know how a wood turner's lathe works, and how to figure out how fast it is turning.
  4. Know how an automatic wood-turning lathe works.
  5. Turn one project on a faceplate and one between centers.
  6. Make matching parts for a project, such as four legs for a table, or two candlesticks or bud vases.
  7. Know how French polish is applied.

Source: Handbook for Boys (1940), summarized

Timeline

What do the patch types mean?
Type A
Square (1911 - 1933). Embroidered on tan cloth and cut into squares. The Scout folded the edges under when sewing it on.
Type B
Wide border crimped (1934 - 1935). The maker folded the edge under and pressed it round, leaving a wide cloth border. Made on depression cloth and heavyweight cloth.
Type C
Tan narrow border crimped (1936 - 1942). The same crimped edge with a narrow border of about 1/8 inch, on tan cloth.
Type D
Fine twill (1942 - 1946). Wartime badges on lighter, finer-weave cloth (fine twill and war cloth) while heavier cloth went to the war effort.
Type E
Khaki narrow border crimped (1947 - 1960). Back to heavier cloth after the war, now in khaki.

Dates are approximate and can vary by badge.
