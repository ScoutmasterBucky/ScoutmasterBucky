The Story

The Automobiling merit badge was retired at the end 1961 and replaced with Automotive Safety.

1911 requirements

  1. Demonstrate how to start a motor, explaining what precautions should be taken.
  2. Take off and put on pneumatic tires.
  3. Know the functions of the clutch, carburetor, valves, magneto, spark plug, differential cam shaft, and different speed gears, and be able to explain difference between a two and four-cycle motor.
  4. Know how to put out burning gasoline or oil.
  5. Have satisfactorily passed the requirements to receive a license to operate an automobile in the community in which he lives.

Source: The Official Handbook for Boys (1911)

Timeline

1911 The Automobiling merit badge is introduced. It is one of the original 57 merit badges.

1912 The first Automobiling merit badges are earned: 16 that year.

1920 The earliest known Automobiling pamphlet printing (Type 1C).

1921 Best ranking: 22nd of 61 merit badges.

1931 All-time high: 7,930 Automobiling merit badges earned.

1961 Last year the Automobiling merit badge is offered; it is replaced by Automotive Safety.

What do the patch types mean?
Type A
Square (1911 - 1933). Embroidered on tan cloth and cut into squares. The Scout folded the edges under when sewing it on.
Type B
Wide border crimped (1934 - 1935). The maker folded the edge under and pressed it round, leaving a wide cloth border. Made on depression cloth and heavyweight cloth.
Type C
Tan narrow border crimped (1936 - 1942). The same crimped edge with a narrow border of about 1/8 inch, on tan cloth.
Type D
Fine twill (1942 - 1946). Wartime badges on lighter, finer-weave cloth (fine twill and war cloth) while heavier cloth went to the war effort.
Type E
Khaki narrow border crimped (1947 - 1960). Back to heavier cloth after the war, now in khaki.
Type F
Rolled edge twill (1961 - 1968). The embroidered border is rolled over the edge instead of crimped, with a gauze backing.
Type G
Cloth back (1961 - 1971). Fully embroidered with a cloth back. By about 1969 every merit badge was made this way.

Dates are approximate and can vary by badge.
