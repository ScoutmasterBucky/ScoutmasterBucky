The Story

The Stalking merit badge was one of four centennial merit badges that were brought out of retirement and offered in 2010 in celebration of the BSA's 100-year anniversary. Due to current societal negative associations with the word "stalking", the 2010-version was renamed Tracking. While the requirements were the same as a historical Stalking merit badge, there was no printed copy of the requirements in pamphlet form.

1911 requirements

  1. Take a series of twenty photographs of wild animals or birds from life, and develop and print them.
  2. Make a group of sixty species of wild flowers, ferns, or grasses, dried and mounted in a book and correctly named.
  3. Make colored drawings of twenty flowers, ferns, or grasses, or twelve sketches from life of animals or birds, original sketches as well as the finished pictures to be submitted.

Source: The Official Handbook for Boys (1911)

Timeline

1911 The Stalking merit badge is introduced. It is one of the original 57 merit badges.

1912 The first Stalking merit badge is earned. Best ranking: 48th of 51 merit badges.

1920 The earliest known Stalking pamphlet printing (Type 1C).

1952 All-time high: 718 Stalking merit badges earned. Last year the Stalking merit badge is offered.

2010 Stalking returns for one year for Scouting's 100th anniversary, renamed Tracking because of the modern meaning of the word "stalking."

What do the patch types mean?
Type A
Square (1911 - 1933). Embroidered on tan cloth and cut into squares. The Scout folded the edges under when sewing it on.
Type B
Wide border crimped (1934 - 1935). The maker folded the edge under and pressed it round, leaving a wide cloth border. Made on depression cloth and heavyweight cloth.
Type C
Tan narrow border crimped (1936 - 1942). The same crimped edge with a narrow border of about 1/8 inch, on tan cloth.
Type D
Fine twill (1942 - 1946). Wartime badges on lighter, finer-weave cloth (fine twill and war cloth) while heavier cloth went to the war effort.
Type E
Khaki narrow border crimped (1947 - 1960). Back to heavier cloth after the war, now in khaki.
Type K
BSA 100th anniversary back (2010 - 2014). The BSA 2010 anniversary logo printed on the back.

Dates are approximate and can vary by badge.
