The Story

The Agriculture merit badge, despite its official retirement in 1975, was phased out slowly as Scouts were allowed to finish the badge if they had already started it prior to the 1975 end of life date. This is evidenced by the following years' earned merit badge counts.

1911 requirements

  1. State different tests with grains.
  2. Grow at least an acre of corn which produces 25 per cent. better than the general average.
  3. Be able to identify and describe common weeds of the community and tell how best to eliminate them.
  4. Be able to identify the common insects and tell how best to handle them.
  5. Have a practical knowledge of plowing, cultivating, drilling, hedging, and draining.
  6. Have a working knowledge of farm machinery, haymaking, reaping, loading, and stacking.
  7. Have a general acquaintance of the routine seasonal work on the farm, including the care of cattle, horses, sheep, and pigs.
  8. Have a knowledge of Campbell's Soil Culture principle, and a knowledge of dry farming and of irrigation farming.

Source: The Official Handbook for Boys (1911)

Timeline

1911 The Agriculture merit badge is introduced. It is one of the original 57 merit badges.

1912 The first Agriculture merit badges are earned: 6 that year.

1920 The earliest known Agriculture pamphlet printing (Type 1C).

1924 Best ranking: 35th of 64 merit badges.

1973 All-time high: 4,130 Agriculture merit badges earned.

1975 Last year the Agriculture merit badge is offered; it is replaced by Animal Science and Plant Science.

What do the patch types mean?
Type A
Square (1911 - 1933). Embroidered on tan cloth and cut into squares. The Scout folded the edges under when sewing it on.
Type B
Wide border crimped (1934 - 1935). The maker folded the edge under and pressed it round, leaving a wide cloth border. Made on depression cloth and heavyweight cloth.
Type C
Tan narrow border crimped (1936 - 1942). The same crimped edge with a narrow border of about 1/8 inch, on tan cloth.
Type D
Fine twill (1942 - 1946). Wartime badges on lighter, finer-weave cloth (fine twill and war cloth) while heavier cloth went to the war effort.
Type E
Khaki narrow border crimped (1947 - 1960). Back to heavier cloth after the war, now in khaki.
Type F
Rolled edge twill (1961 - 1968). The embroidered border is rolled over the edge instead of crimped, with a gauze backing.
Type G
Cloth back (1961 - 1971). Fully embroidered with a cloth back. By about 1969 every merit badge was made this way.
Type H
Plastic back (1972 - 2002). A plastic stiffener on the back. Collectors tell them apart by the back, which can be clear, white, or a very light blue plastic, with waffle-textured and smooth molded versions. These are sorted into variations H1 through H5.

Dates are approximate and can vary by badge.
