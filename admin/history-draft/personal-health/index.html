1911 requirements

  1. Write a statement on the care of the teeth.
  2. State a principle to govern in eating, and state in the order of their importance, five rules to govern the care of his health.
  3. Be able to tell the difference in effect of a cold and hot bath.
  4. Describe the effect of alcohol and tobacco on the growing boy.
  5. Tell how to care for the feet on a march.
  6. Describe a good healthful game and state its merit.
  7. Describe the effects of walking as an exercise.
  8. Tell how athletics may be overdone.

Source: The Official Handbook for Boys (1911)

Timeline

1911 The Personal Health merit badge is introduced. It is one of the original 57 merit badges. The first Personal Health merit badges are earned: 15 that year. Best ranking: 1st of 22 merit badges.

1919 The earliest known Personal Health pamphlet printing (Type 1C).

1944 All-time high: 47,820 Personal Health merit badges earned.

1952 Last year the Personal Health merit badge is offered.

What do the patch types mean?
Type A
Square (1911 - 1933). Embroidered on tan cloth and cut into squares. The Scout folded the edges under when sewing it on.
Type B
Wide border crimped (1934 - 1935). The maker folded the edge under and pressed it round, leaving a wide cloth border. Made on depression cloth and heavyweight cloth.
Type C
Tan narrow border crimped (1936 - 1942). The same crimped edge with a narrow border of about 1/8 inch, on tan cloth.
Type D
Fine twill (1942 - 1946). Wartime badges on lighter, finer-weave cloth (fine twill and war cloth) while heavier cloth went to the war effort.
Type E
Khaki narrow border crimped (1947 - 1960). Back to heavier cloth after the war, now in khaki.

Dates are approximate and can vary by badge.
