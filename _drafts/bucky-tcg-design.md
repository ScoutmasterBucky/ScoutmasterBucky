# Bucky's Trail — card game design (draft 1)

Working title. Alternates: *Badge Quest*, *Sash*, *Pack It In*.

One card set, three rulesets that nest. A 6-year-old and a 16-year-old can sit at the same table: the young player uses the color and the star, the older player uses the cost, stats and ability text on the very same card.

The same deck is also a second game: a planning game that runs a real Patrol Leaders' Council and ends with a plan the troop can use (section 15). Two games in one box.

## 1. What the game is about

You are a Scout filling a merit badge sash. Badges are the cards you collect. Helpers, Gear and Events speed you up or slow the table down. Reaching rank milestones gives you power. Nobody ever loses a badge they have earned ("earned is earned"), so younger players are never wiped out.

Why this works for the brand: the site already has Bucky art for all 142 merit badges and an Eagle-required flag on each one. That is the entire core card pool, drawn and named already.

## 2. The six Trails (suits)

Every badge card belongs to one Trail. Color plus icon, so non-readers can match.

| Trail | Color | Icon | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Outdoors | green | tent | Camping, Hiking, Backpacking, Wilderness Survival |
| Nature & Science | blue | leaf/atom | Environmental Science, Astronomy, Chemistry, Insect Study |
| Citizenship & Community | red | flag | Citizenship in the Nation, Communication, Law, American Heritage |
| Life & Home | gold | house | Personal Management, Cooking, Family Life, Personal Fitness |
| Arts & Trades | purple | hammer/brush | Woodwork, Art, Photography, Welding, Game Design |
| Sports & Water | teal | wave | Swimming, Lifesaving, Canoeing, Athletics, Snow Sports |

Eagle-required badges get a gold star in the corner in addition to their Trail.

- **Tier 1 players** read only the band color and the star.
- **Tier 2 players** add Effort cost and ability text.
- **Tier 3 players** add the three stats for Challenges.

## 4. Tier 1 — Campfire (ages 5+, 2–6 players, 10 minutes)

Badge cards only. Everything else stays in the box.

**Sash.** Deal 5. On your turn draw 1, then play 1 badge face up into your sash. When anyone has 7 badges in their sash the round ends. Score your sash: 1 badge of a Trail = 1, a pair = 3, three or more = 6. Each gold star adds 1. Highest wins.

**Trail Fish.** Go Fish by Trail color. Four of a Trail is a "patrol". Most patrols wins. For the youngest players.

Both teach the Trail colors and the star, which is all a child needs to join a Tier 2 table later.

## 5. Tier 2 — Trail (ages 8+, 2–4 players, 20–30 minutes)

Add Helpers, Gear, Events, Weather and the Rank cards. Everyone plays from one shared deck, or each player uses a pre-built 40-card Trail deck.

**Turn.**
1. Gain Effort equal to your rank level (everyone starts at 2). Unspent Effort carries over, but never above 2.
2. Draw 2.
3. Play as many cards as you can pay for. Badges go to your sash. Helpers and Gear stay in your camp. Events resolve and are discarded.

**Counselors.** You cannot earn a merit badge without a merit badge counselor, in the program or in the game. A badge may be earned only if a Counselor of its Trail is **available to the troop**: on the table's Counselor list, in any player's camp, held as the Merit Badge Counselor adult Position (section 16), or named on a Scoutmaster Bucky Team Member card in play (section 19). Scoutmaster Bucky counts for every Trail. Counselors serve the whole table; the player whose camp holds one also gets its discount of 1 on that Trail (minimum 1). One Counselor of each Trail per camp.

- **Counselor list.** At setup, deal 3 Counselors face up to the middle of the table. They stay all game and serve everyone. Merit Badge Day and Summer Camp Adventures put all six on the list.
- **Find a counselor.** Spend your whole turn to search the deck for a Counselor of a Trail with none available and put it on the list. The troop had to make a phone call.
- The only badge you can ever add to your sash without a counselor at the table is one you earned in real life: an EARNED foil (section 7).

**Ranks.** Milestones scale the real ratios down so a game ends in one sitting. The first four ranks are earned by demonstrating Skills (section 17), not by badges, exactly as in the program. The last three are earned by badges and need First Class first.

| Rank | Board tests to pass | Badges in sash | of which Eagle-required | Effort per turn | Also |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scout | none: the floor everyone stands on | | | 2 | |
| Tenderfoot | 1 | | | 2 | hand size +1 |
| Second Class | 2 | | | 2 | Weather costs you 1 less |
| First Class | 3 | | | 3 | unlocks Star; needed to set out on a Trek |
| Star | | 4 | 2 | 3 | |
| Life | | 7 | 4 | 4 | |
| Eagle | | 10 | 6 | wins the game | |

Hitting a milestone is public: flip the Rank card to your side of the table. The first four ranks are proven at a Board of Review, never written down, and can be lost on a bad day (section 17); in Summit the tests are 2, 3 and 4. Star, Life and Eagle are never lost once flipped, but you must be First Class at the moment you flip Star.

**Abilities.** Every badge has two lines of ability text, and both come from what the badge is about.

- **Earned** (one time, when it enters your sash): short and mostly draw, discount, or protect. Camping: draw a card. Communication: look at the top 3 cards, keep 1. Cooking: each other player gives you 1 card from hand; you give each back a card of your choice.
- **Worn** (ongoing, only while the badge is one of the three you wear, section 20): the badge's theme as a standing skill. Camping: Weather costs you 1 less, because your campsite is better. Cooking: +1 Effort every third turn, because your patrol eats well. First Aid: once per game, cancel a Weather card played on anyone. Personal Management: carry-over Effort limit +2.

You wear one Eagle-required badge and two electives at a time, so no more than three Worn abilities are ever live for one player. That cap is what keeps the game readable at 142 badges.

## 6. Tier 3 — Summit (ages 12+, 2 players or 2v2, 30–45 minutes)

For the players who want a real TCG.

**Deck construction.** 40 cards. At least 20 badges, no more than 2 copies of any badge, badges from no more than 3 Trails. Your Oath (one Core virtue and two Secondary virtues, section 10) sits outside the deck and is your character. Preconstructed starters are legal as-is, so a beginner's deck is always tournament-legal.

**Challenges.** Camporee and a few abilities start a Challenge. The challenger names one stat (Hands, Know, Care) and one Trail. Each player totals that stat across their sash badges in that Trail. The winner takes a Ribbon. Three Ribbons count as an Eagle Palm, which is an alternate win condition. Losing a Challenge costs nothing but the Ribbon, so the "earned is earned" promise holds.

**Your Oath is your persona.** The Core virtue you choose gives the deck its identity, and the two Secondary virtues tune it. Twelve Cores times 55 possible Secondary pairs is 660 Oaths, far more archetypes than a handful of persona cards would give. Details in section 10.

**Formats.** Swiss rounds, best of 3, 40-minute rounds. 2v2 shares a sash between partners, which is how an older sibling carries a younger one at an event.

## 7. Real-world tie-in (the part no other TCG can do)

- **Earn it.** A Scout who completes a badge at a Bucky event gets that badge's card with a foil "EARNED" stamp and the event date. That is the rarity system: a foil Welding card means you actually welded. Cannot be bought. In collection mode an EARNED foil is also the one badge that needs no counselor at the table: up to 3 foils may start in your sash at setup, and every foil you own counts at the Court of Honor for the collection score. The counselor was Bucky, at the event.
- **Print-and-play.** Full set as a free PDF on the site, same way the workbooks ship. Cheap to start, no inventory, and it is content the site already hosts.
- **Card back QR** links to the badge's page and workbook, so the card is also a study aid.
- **Merit badge day warm-up.** Campfire rules fill the gathering area before class and the early-finish gap (fits the existing "early finish goes to the gathering area" practice).

## 8. Magic / Pokémon compatibility, honestly

You cannot sell or distribute cards that present themselves as Magic or Pokémon expansions. The mana symbols, energy symbols, card frames and names are trademarks of Wizards of the Coast and The Pokémon Company, and both enforce them. Printing them invites a cease-and-desist and would drag the Bucky brand into it.

What you *can* do, and what section 3 is built for:

- **Universal stat strip.** Every badge already carries Effort (a cost), plus Hands / Know / Care (1–5). Those read naturally as cost / power / toughness / a third number in any kitchen-table game. Nothing on the card names another game.
- **Printed conversion sheet.** One page in the rulebook: "To use Bucky cards in a Magic-style game: cost = Effort in generic mana, power = Hands, toughness = Know, enters with an ability. To use them in a Pokémon-style game: HP = Care × 10, attack = Hands × 10, retreat = Effort." Players house-rule it. That is legal because it is rules text about your own cards, not use of their marks.
- **Familiar feel.** Tier 3 uses cost, stats, a persona and 40-card decks on purpose. A Magic or Pokémon player sits down and already knows the grammar.

Recommendation: build the game to stand on its own, ship the conversion sheet as a bonus, and never print another company's symbols.

## 9. Scouting America licensing (must settle before selling)

Merit badge emblem designs, "Eagle Scout", the fleur-de-lis, "BSA" and "Scouting America" are registered marks. The site's existing rule already applies: Bucky-only art, no emblems. For a **free** print-and-play used at your own events, badge names and rank names are low risk. For anything **sold**, contact Scouting America's licensing program first; they license products to approved vendors and the card game would need that. Plan the first release as free so the game can exist while that conversation happens.

## 10. The Scout Law: your Oath (12 cards)

Twelve Law cards, one per point, Bucky living that point in the art. Before a Trail or Summit game, every player builds an **Oath**: one **Core** virtue and two **Secondary** virtues, placed on a small Oath mat with three slots. Every player holds their own set of twelve, so nothing is scarce; the choice is the game.

Each Law card does three different things depending on where it sits:

1. **Core power**: an always-on effect that shapes your whole game. Unique to that point.
2. **Secondary power**: a smaller, once-per-game effect. Also unique to that point, and different from its Core power.
3. **Pillar**: each point belongs to one of four Pillars. Your three chosen cards' Pillars add up into a combination bonus, so every possible Oath gets a combo without a lookup table.

Each card also keeps its **living trigger**: an act at the table that shows the virtue. Living a virtue counts toward Scout Spirit (below).

### The four Pillars

The twelve points fall into four groups of three. Each Pillar has one bonus that scales with how many of your three Oath cards come from it.

| Pillar | Points | Bonus per card from this Pillar in your Oath |
|---|---|---|
| **Honor** | Trustworthy, Loyal, Obedient | Carry-over Effort limit +1 each |
| **Fellowship** | Helpful, Friendly, Courteous | Hand size +1 each |
| **Heart** | Kind, Cheerful, Reverent | Weather costs you 1 less each (3 = immune) |
| **Grit** | Thrifty, Brave, Clean | Badges with Effort 3 cost 1 less each (minimum 1) |

Because an Oath is three cards and a Pillar holds three, only three shapes exist, and each gets a name:

| Oath shape | Pillars | Extra |
|---|---|---|
| **Focused** | all 3 from one Pillar (the whole Pillar) | that Pillar's bonus at level 3 |
| **Leaning** | 2 + 1 | one Pillar at level 2, one at level 1 |
| **Well-rounded** | 1 + 1 + 1 | three Pillars at level 1, and you start the game with one Scout Spirit credit |

Focused is strong in one lane and is the only way to reach level 3. Well-rounded is a little of three things plus a head start on rank. Leaning sits between. The intent is that none wins more often than the others; see the balance rules below.

### The twelve cards

| Point | Pillar | Core power (always on) | Secondary power (once per game) | Living trigger |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Trustworthy | Honor | At the start of your turn, name a badge in your hand. If you earn it this turn, draw 1. | Reveal your hand; draw 1 for each Trail shown (max 3). | Earn a badge you named |
| Loyal | Honor | Badges of your largest Trail cost 1 less (min 1). | Take a badge of your largest Trail from your deck or the discard pile into your hand. | Earn a third badge in one Trail |
| Obedient | Honor | Events cost you 1 less. | When an Event tells every player to do something, you do it twice. | Finish an all-player Event instruction first |
| Helpful | Fellowship | Whenever you give a card to another player, draw 1 (once per turn). | Give each other player a card from your hand; draw 2 per card given. | Give a card to another player |
| Friendly | Fellowship | Whenever another player reaches a rank, draw 1. | Every player draws 1; you draw 2. | Congratulate: play when an opponent ranks up |
| Courteous | Fellowship | You may pass an Event you drew to the next player and draw 1 instead. After a Court of Honor you choose your seat in turn order. | Skip your draw so each other player draws 1; gain 2 Effort. | Pass an Event to another player |
| Kind | Heart | When you cancel Weather on another player, you both draw 1. | Discard all Weather from any one player's camp. | Cancel Weather on another player |
| Cheerful | Heart | If you have the fewest badges at the start of your turn, +1 Effort. | When Weather hits you, draw 3 instead of taking its effect. | Start a turn with the fewest badges |
| Reverent | Heart | Your rest turn (earn no badges) draws 3 and gains 1 Effort. | Take a rest turn: draw 4 and discard all Weather from your camp. | Take a rest turn |
| Thrifty | Grit | Carry-over Effort limit +2. | Turn up to 3 cards from your hand into 3 Effort. | End two turns in a row with unspent Effort |
| Brave | Grit | Tier 3: +1 to every stat total in Challenges. Tier 2: when you earn an Effort-3 badge, draw 1. | Tier 3: start a Challenge at any time for free. Tier 2: earn one Effort-3 badge for 0. | Start a Challenge against a player ahead of you, or earn an Effort-3 badge while behind |
| Clean | Grit | At the end of your turn, if your camp has no Weather, you may discard 1 and draw 1. | Discard your hand and all Weather in your camp; draw 5. | End a turn with no Weather in camp and 3 or fewer cards in hand |

Within each Pillar the three cards cover three different jobs: one leans toward cards, one toward Effort, one toward the table (Events, Weather, other players). That keeps any Pillar from being the "draw Pillar" or the "Effort Pillar" and is the first line of balance.

### Scout Spirit

Real ranks require Scout spirit. So do the game's: Star needs 1 of your three virtues lived, Life 2, Eagle all 3. A living trigger can be met once per virtue per game, and lived virtues are turned face up on the Oath mat so the table can see them. You cannot buy Eagle with badges alone; you have to have lived your whole Oath at the table. If playtesting shows Eagle stalling on a trigger that never comes up (Kind needs Weather on someone else, Friendly needs an opponent to rank up), drop Eagle to 2 before changing any trigger.

In **Trail (Tier 2)** all three powers are active from the start, and living them is only for Scout Spirit. In **Summit (Tier 3)** the Core power is active from the start but each Secondary power switches on only once its virtue has been lived, so the way you play unlocks the deck you built.

### Keeping the twelve equal

- **Power budget.** Every Core power is worth about one card per turn when it fires and fires in most turns. Every Secondary is a once-per-game effect worth about two cards. Pillar bonuses are the only place numbers differ, and the four are pegged to each other: +1 carry-over, +1 hand, 1 less Weather, 1 less on a big badge are all "one Effort or one card".
- **No dead Cores.** Each Core fires in a normal game without needing a specific other card. Kind needs Weather, Friendly needs other players ranking up, so both get a Secondary that works alone.
- **Measure, then nudge.** Record Core and Oath shape for every game at events. Any Core that wins more than its share over 30 games gets its number trimmed; any Core under its share gets a bump. Change one number at a time. Never change what a virtue does, only how much.
- **Cores are not a stat.** Nothing on a Law card is a number another card can grow. That stops combos from running away.

### Tier 1 (Campfire)

Each child picks one Core virtue card before the game and keeps it in their sash. It matches any Trail. That is the whole rule, and it is how they learn the twelve words.

### Variant: the Law Track (event clock)

Stack a thirteenth set of Law cards in order and flip one per round as a table-wide rule (Trustworthy: hands face up; Helpful: when you draw, the player on your left draws 1; Cheerful: everyone gains 1 Effort; Clean: discard all Weather; and so on). Twelve rounds caps game length at events and teaches the Law in order. Optional, printed on the rulebook's back page.

One rule above all: an Oath card is never taken away or changed mid-game. Like a badge, it is yours.

## 11. Legacy badges (the retired ones)

The history data already lists 103 discontinued badges with their years, the badge that replaced each one, and a lifetime rank by how many were ever earned. After collapsing plain renames (Bee Farming / Bee Keeping / Beekeeping, Handicapped / Handicap Awareness, Leatherworking / Leathercraft, the two Hog and two Farm Records badges) that is about 90 distinct Legacy cards. Release them in waves, because none of them has Bucky art yet.

### Look

A sepia frame instead of a Trail color band. A year band under the name ("1919 – 1923") and a lineage line ("Replaced by Radio"). Vintage Bucky in period gear. No patch photos: the emblems are trademarked even when the badge is dead.

### Rarity comes from history, not from a random pack

The fewer Scouts ever earned a badge, the rarer the card. The lifetime rank in the data does this for free.

| Tier | Rule | Members |
|---|---|---|
| **Lost** (rarest) | fewest ever earned, most lived under ten years | Ambulance (1910–11), Ornithology (1911–13), Invention (1911–17), Wireless (1919–23), Food Systems, Farm and Ranch Management, Nut Culture, Airplane Design, Aerodynamics, Aeronautics, Airplane Structure, Agribusiness, Metallurgy, Mining, Stalking, Taxidermy |
| **Rare** | few earned, mostly the farm and shop badges of 1928–1975 | Fruit Culture, Cotton Farming, Foundry Practice, Small Grains, Forage Crops, Landscape Gardening, Seamanship, Rocks and Minerals, Blacksmithing, Consumer Buying, Sheep Farming, Dramatics, Angling, Interpreting, Safety First, Cement Work, Pigeon Raising, General Science, Zoology, Business, Corn Farming, Beef Production, Wood Turning, Citizenship (1948–51), Personal Finances, Agriculture |
| **Uncommon** | earned in real numbers, gone for decades | Botany, Signaling, Medicine, Craftsmanship, Dairying, Cinematography, Conservation, World Brotherhood, Rabbit Raising, Automotive Safety, Masonry, Automobiling, Mechanical Drawing, Machinery, Water Skiing |
| **Common Legacy** | many earned, often within living memory | Physical Development, Skiing, Marksmanship, Wildlife Management, First Aid to Animals, Civics, Bookbinding, Computers, Carpentry, Pathfinding, Handicraft, Aviation (1914–41), Citizenship in the Home, Personal Health, Atomic Energy, Firemanship, Insect Life, Mammals, Printing, Reptile Study, Communications, Poultry Keeping |

A fun twist in the data: Aviation (1914–1941) and Printing share a name with a current badge. A Legacy card can carry the same name as a modern one and be a different card.

### How they play

**Lineage.** Every Legacy card names its successor from the data. A Legacy card attaches to its modern descendant in your sash: Wireless under Radio, Blacksmithing under Metalwork, Computers under Digital Technology, Angling under Fishing. Attached, it counts as a second badge of that Trail toward ranks and sets, and its power turns on. Unattached, it sits in camp as a keepsake and does nothing. So the rarest card in the game is useless until you earn the badge it became, which is the whole lesson. Legacy cards with no successor (Invention, Taxidermy, Pathfinding, Stalking, Pigeon Raising) are **Lost Trail**: they attach to any badge, and they are the only wild Legacy cards, which is why they sit in the rarest tier.

**Powers are once per game and bigger than anything on a modern card.** Flip the card sideways when used. Examples:

| Legacy card | Power |
|---|---|
| Wireless | Morse: at any time, even on another player's turn, draw 2 |
| Signaling | Look at any player's hand |
| Pathfinding | Search your deck for any badge and put it in your hand |
| Invention | Copy the ability of any badge on the table for the rest of the game |
| Stalking | Take the top card of another player's deck |
| Ambulance | Move a Weather card from any camp to the discard pile |
| Firemanship | Discard every Weather card on the table |
| Bee Keeping | Honey: gain 1 extra Effort every turn while attached (ongoing, not once) |
| Taxidermy | Return a badge from the discard pile to your hand |
| Atomic Energy | Double your Effort this turn |
| Computers | Draw up to your hand size |
| Interpreting | Swap a Counselor in your camp for any Counselor in the discard pile |
| Automobiling | Take an extra turn after this one |
| Citizenship (1948–51) | Counts as any one Eagle-required badge for rank purposes |

**Limits that keep them from taking over.** Tier 2: no more than 2 Legacy cards per player in play. Tier 3: no more than 3 in a 40-card deck, one copy each, and the Lost tier is limited to 1.

**Tier 1.** A Legacy card is worth 2 points and matches any Trail. A child holding one has treasure.

### How Scouts get them

Nobody can earn a retired badge, so the EARNED foil does not apply. Instead a Legacy card is **Discovered**:

- Answer a question from the badge's history page at an event (the history pages are the question bank).
- A prize at a Court of Honor or the Test Lab Day.
- One Lost-tier card hidden in each merit badge day's gathering area. First Scout to find it keeps it.
- Print-and-play: Legacy cards live in a separate "Vault" PDF, released one wave at a time so there is always a new one to look for.

The card back QR goes to the badge's history page, so the rare card is also the way a Scout learns a badge existed.

## 12. Adventures, meetings and quests (the paths)

A Scout's year is not one kind of day. The game should not be one kind of game. The list of paths sorts into three layers plus a campaign that strings them together.

| Layer | What it is in the game | From the list |
|---|---|---|
| **Adventure** | one large card that frames a whole session: length, terrain, hazards, a twist, a reward | campout, high adventure trek, field trip, summer camp, council event (camporee), merit badge day |
| **Meeting** | the rhythm and the governance of play: rounds, team talk, and who decides what comes next | troop meeting, patrol meeting, PLC, district and council |
| **Quest** | a goal card: secret and personal, or public and shared | individual quests, group quests |
| **Campaign** | a sequence of Adventures with a sash that persists between sessions | a Scouting year |

### Adventures (one per game)

Place one Adventure card in the middle before dealing. It changes the table for that game only. Every Adventure has the same five lines so players read them the same way:

- **Length**: rounds until the Court of Honor ends the game (if nobody reaches Eagle first).
- **Terrain**: which Trails cost 1 less, or 1 more.
- **Hazards**: which Weather cards go into the deck, and how many.
- **Twist**: one rule unique to this Adventure.
- **Reward**: an end-of-game bonus tied to the theme.

| Adventure | Length | Terrain | Hazards | Twist | Reward |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Weekend Campout** | 8 | Outdoors −1 | Rainstorm ×2, Mosquitoes | Each round starts with a Campfire: the player with the fewest badges draws 1 | Most Outdoors badges: a Ribbon |
| **High Adventure Trek** | 12 | Outdoors and Sports & Water −1, everything else +1 | Lost Trail, Rainstorm, Blisters | Crew rule: you must be First Class to "set out"; players who are not by round 4 get a Crew Leader gift of 2 cards. Every round each player pays 1 Effort trail toll or skips a draw | The crew wins together if the table's combined Outdoors and Water badges reach 15 by the end; the player with the most of them is Crew Leader and takes 2 Ribbons |
| **Field Trip** | 6 | the host (whoever reveals the card) names one Trail: −1 for everyone | none | The Exhibit: four badges face up in the middle; on your turn you may earn one from the Exhibit instead of from your hand, then refill. Introduces an open market | Most badges from the named Trail: a Ribbon |
| **Summer Camp** | 12 | none | Thunderstorm (sends everyone to the dining hall: no badges this round), Homesick | Morning and afternoon sessions: you may earn 2 badges per turn. Counselors cost 0 | Every player with 6 or more badges draws a Legacy card (the camp trading post) |
| **Merit Badge Day** | 3 long rounds ("sessions") | none | none | Class selection: each session every player secretly picks one badge from hand and reveals at once. If two players pick the same badge the class is full; the one with more unspent Effort gets it and the other draws 2. Mirrors class limits at a real event | Any badge earned here is "EARNED" for the campaign record |
| **Camporee (council event)** | 8 | none | Rainstorm | A Challenge every round, the stat and Trail chosen by rotating Patrol Leaders. Patrols of two if 4 or more players | Most Ribbons takes the Camporee flag: counts as a rank step |
| **Service Project** | 8 | Citizenship & Community −1 | none | A Group Quest is always on the table (below), and completing it ends the game early with a Court of Honor | Everyone who contributed gains a Scout Spirit credit |
| **Troop Meeting** (the default) | 10 | none | one of each | none | none. This is the plain Trail game from section 5 |

Twelve Adventures is enough for a first set; the eight above plus Winter Campout, Day Hike, Conservation Weekend and Court of Honor Night.

### Meetings (the rhythm)

- **Troop Meeting** is a round. Nothing to learn; it is the word the rulebook uses for "everyone has taken a turn".
- **Patrol Meeting** is the team phase. In 2v2 or in any game of 4 or more, players form patrols. At the start of each round a patrol gets 30 seconds of open talk and may pass one card between partners. Table talk is legal inside a patrol and not across patrols. The patrol method is how an older player carries a younger one without playing for them.
- **PLC** is how the table chooses the next Adventure. Draw three Adventure cards. Every player who holds a Rank card votes; ties go to the Senior Patrol Leader, who is the player with the most badges in the last game (first game: the youngest player). Youth-led, on the table. It also appears as an Event card inside a game: "PLC: reveal two Twists; the table votes which one applies next round".
- **District and Council** are the event layers, not cards. A **District Roundtable** is a league night: several tables, each a different Adventure, results on one sheet. A **Council Event** is the big day: Camporee Adventure on every table, patrols drawn across troops, and the Camporee flag as the prize. These are formats for Bucky's merit badge days and Test Lab Days to run, with the game's own words for them.

### Quests (the goals)

**Individual Quests** are secret. Deal 2 at the start, keep 1, put it face down under your Oath mat. Score it at the Court of Honor if complete. Unfinished quests score 0 and never cost anything, because the game never punishes a Scout for aiming high.

| Individual Quest | Done when | Worth |
|---|---|---|
| Naturalist | 3 Nature & Science badges in your sash | a Ribbon |
| Eagle-bound | 4 Eagle-required badges | a Ribbon |
| Specialist | 3 badges with Effort 3 | a Ribbon |
| Jack of all Trails | one badge from each of the six Trails | 2 Ribbons |
| Mentor | you have given 3 cards to other players | a Ribbon and a Scout Spirit credit |
| Historian | a Legacy card attached to its successor | a Ribbon |
| Quartermaster | 3 Gear cards in your camp | a Ribbon |
| Iron Scout | reach Life with no Counselor in camp | 2 Ribbons |

Twenty in the set; the rest follow the same pattern (one per Trail, one per Pillar, a few odd ones).

**Group Quests** are public and shared. One sits on the Adventure card (the Service Project Adventure always has one; others draw one at random). Everyone can contribute, the whole table is rewarded when it completes, and the player who places the last piece gets a little extra. This is cooperative play inside a competitive game, and it is why a losing player still has something to do.

| Group Quest | Done when | Everyone gets | Finisher gets |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eagle Project | the table has discarded 8 cards onto the project (any player, any turn, 1 card per turn) | draw 2 | a Scout Spirit credit |
| Conservation Day | 6 Nature & Science badges across all sashes | all Weather discarded | a Ribbon |
| Flag Ceremony | every player has at least one Citizenship & Community badge | each player gains 1 Effort | a Ribbon |
| Troop Campout | every player has at least 3 badges | the player with the fewest badges draws 3 | nothing; the reward is the table catching up |
| Food Drive | 10 Life & Home or Citizenship badges across all sashes | each player draws 1 | a Ribbon |

**Crew mode** (fully cooperative) is the same cards with the Weather deck as the opponent: the table plays the High Adventure Trek together and wins or loses as one. For families with very young players, and for a Scout who wants to play alone.

### Campaign: a Scouting Year

Six to eight sessions, each one Adventure, chosen by PLC vote at the end of the previous session. Each player keeps a one-page Scoutbook sheet: badges earned (a tick per badge, EARNED stamps noted), ranks reached, Legacy cards discovered, Scout Spirit lived. The sash persists; a new game starts with the badges from the sheet as a head start on rank. A player who joins mid-year starts at the lowest rank anyone holds, so nobody is shut out. The year ends with the Court of Honor Night Adventure, which is pure scoring plus one last round for everyone to finish a quest.

The Scoutbook sheet is also the thing a Scout brings to a merit badge day to get an EARNED stamp recorded, which ties the campaign to the real events.

### Which tier gets what

| | Campfire (Tier 1) | Trail (Tier 2) | Summit (Tier 3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adventures | none | one per game, chosen by the host | PLC vote, all twelve legal |
| Patrols | no | optional in 4+ | 2v2 standard |
| Individual Quests | no | optional | standard, 2 dealt keep 1 |
| Group Quests | no | one per game | one per game |
| Campaign | no | yes, with the Scoutbook sheet | yes, and it feeds league standings |

## 13. More of the Scouting year

A pass through troop calendars, service traditions, ceremonies and Scouting history turned up more content than the Adventures in section 12 cover. It sorts into five kinds of card. Names marked † are Scouting America program names or marks; fine in the free print-and-play under the section 9 rule, to be checked before anything is sold.

### Good Turn cards (12): "Do a Good Turn Daily"

The slogan is older than the merit badge program, and the 1909 story of the London Scout who refused a tip is the founding story of American Scouting. Scouts once carried a Good Turn coin and flipped it when the day's good turn was done. In the game:

- **Campfire (Tier 1):** a Good Turn card is a wild. It matches any Trail. Same job as the Core virtue card, so the youngest players see "good turns go with everything".
- **Trail and Summit:** a Good Turn is an **instant**. It can be played at any time, costs 0, and always does something for another player first. Most also do something small for you. Twelve of them in the deck, drawn like any other card.
- **The coin.** Each player has a Good Turn coin token, dull side up at the start of each round. The first time you play a Good Turn in a round, flip it bright side up. At the end of the round every bright coin's owner draws 1. Playing a good turn every day pays, which is the whole point.

| Good Turn | Effect |
|---|---|
| Help across the street | The player with the fewest badges gains 1 Effort. You draw 1. |
| Hold the door | The next player draws 1 before their turn. You gain 1 Effort. |
| Share your lunch | Give a card to any player. Both of you draw 1. |
| Carry the pack | Move one Weather card from another player's camp to yours. It costs you nothing while Carried. |
| Lend your compass | Another player may earn a badge from the Exhibit or discard pile this turn. |
| Pick up litter | Discard one Weather from the table. Everyone gains 1 Effort. |
| Teach a knot | Another player's next badge costs 1 less. |
| Visit a neighbor | The player who has gone longest without earning a badge draws 2. |
| Give blood | Lose 1 Effort. Every other player gains 1. Counts as living Helpful. |
| Return the wallet | Take a card from the discard pile and give it to the player who discarded it. |
| Shovel the walk | Cancel a Weather card as it is played on anyone, including you. |
| **The Unknown Scout** | Play it face down. Resolve any Good Turn in the discard pile again. Nobody is told which. Counts as living any one virtue of your choice. |

The Unknown Scout is the one Good Turn with no name on it, which is the story.

### Heritage cards (~12): knowing the history

A Scout who knows where Scouting came from should get something for it. ## 14. Roles: the Scoutmaster, the Senior Patrol Leader and the opening PLC

Dungeons & Dragons has a Dungeon Master who runs the world and never plays to win. Scouting has a better version of that already: the **Senior Patrol Leader** runs the troop, and the **Scoutmaster** advises from the back of the room. The game uses both, in those proportions. Three rules frame everything below:

1. **Every role is optional.** Two Scouts and a deck can play. The Weather deck runs the Trail by itself when nobody holds it. Unlike D&D, the game never requires a referee.
2. **Youth-led.** When both roles are filled, the SPL runs the table and the Scoutmaster only advises, narrates and checks in. The Scoutmaster never overrules a PLC decision.
3. **Roles matter for the group parts.** Ranks, badges and Individual Quests are each player's own. The roles exist for the things the table does together: the PLC, Group Quests, Crew mode, patrols and the campaign.

### The Senior Patrol Leader (a player, usually)

Elected at the opening PLC by show of hands; in a campaign, re-elected every third session (a six-month term, like a troop). The SPL:

- Runs the opening PLC and keeps the PLC sheet.
- Keeps turn order, reads the Adventure's Twist aloud, breaks ties, and runs Group Quests (announces progress, places contributed cards, calls completion).
- Holds the **sealed Twists** (below) when there is no Scoutmaster.
- Names the stat and Trail for Camporee Challenges, rotating to Patrol Leaders when patrols are formed.

**Does the SPL play?** At tables of 5 or fewer, yes: the SPL has a sash and an Oath like everyone else, and the job is only procedure. At tables of 6 or more, the SPL sits out of the sash race and runs the table outright, D&D-style, with the SPL's own reward being a Scout Spirit credit and a Legacy card at the end. Rotating the job means every Scout runs a table sometime, which is the point of the position in real life.

**Patrol Leaders.** When patrols form, each elects a PL who runs the 30-second Patrol Meeting and makes the patrol's calls (sled stations at Klondike, who answers a trivia question). Assistant SPL is the SPL's partner in 2v2.

### The Scoutmaster (an adult, optional)

Never has a sash and never wins. The Scoutmaster's Handbook is the "DM screen". The job has five tools, all of them from the real program:

| Tool | What it does at the table | Real-world root |
|---|---|---|
| **The Trail hand** | In Crew mode (fully cooperative) the Scoutmaster plays the Trail: holds the Weather deck as a hand of 3, with a budget of 1 Trail Effort per player per round, and chooses what hits whom and when. Without a Scoutmaster, Weather is drawn blind. | The DM's monsters, but it is the weather and the terrain, never another Scout |
| **Sealed Twists** | Ten face-down cards, one or two chosen per Adventure at the PLC and revealed at the Scoutmaster's call: "Thunderstorm rolls in at round 5", "A lost hiker: the first patrol to spend 3 Effort finds them and takes a Ribbon", "Trading post opens: Legacy cards may be bought for 4 Effort this round", "Camp inspection: tidiest camp (fewest Weather) draws 2". Narrated, not just read. | Nobody knows what the weekend will bring |
| **Scoutmaster conference** | Required before every Board of Review (section 17), and the Scoutmaster's only part in advancement, because Scoutmasters and Assistant Scoutmasters never sit on a board. A private minute: one question about a skill the Scout holds and one about a virtue lived. The Scoutmaster may also use it to hand a struggling player a hint or a Scout Spirit credit, or a runaway leader a Group Quest to carry. Nobody fails a conference; it ends with "you're ready" or "let's practice one more", which is a one-turn wait. | The conference before every rank |
| **The Board calendar** | Decides when a requested board happens: immediate if a committee seat is free, scheduled if not. May find a committee member early once per round for a Scout stuck at Scout rank, may delay one per player per game, may never refuse one or delay a board another Scout yielded a turn for (section 17). The Scoutmaster does not sit on the board. | Boards wait for committee members; the Scoutmaster finds them |
| **Scoutmaster's Minute** | The last thing at the table. The Scoutmaster names one Good Turn, one brave play and one lived virtue seen that game, by player. The named players each draw a Legacy or Heritage card for their collection. Then the game is over. | The closing minute of every troop meeting |

The Scoutmaster also reads Heritage trivia when present, and holds the Scoutbook sheets between campaign sessions.

What the Scoutmaster does not do: decide the Adventure, change a Twist after it is sealed, touch a player's hand, or settle rules disputes the SPL has already settled. The Handbook says so on page one.

### The opening PLC (five minutes, before the first card is dealt)

The PLC sheet is a one-page agenda the SPL fills in, in order. It is printed like a real PLC agenda and sets the format and the constraints of what follows.

| Agenda item | Decision | Options |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Roll call | Who is at the table; elect the SPL by show of hands; name a Scoutmaster if an adult wants the job | |
| 2. Extent | How much are we playing? | One game · Short campaign (3 sessions) · Scouting Year (8 sessions) |
| 3. Tier | Which rules? | Campfire · Trail · Summit. **Floor rule:** the tier is the highest one every player at the table is comfortable with. Older players still get their depth from their own Oath and Individual Quests, which are per player |
| 4. Adventure | Which one first? | Vote from 3 drawn, or follow the calendar (section 13) |
| 5. Constraints | House rules, checked off | Time limit (rounds or minutes) · Weather on/off · Legacy and Heritage on/off · Patrols or individual · Competitive or Crew mode · Number of Group Quests (0 to 2) · Sealed Twists (0 to 2) · Trading allowed between players (yes/no) |
| 6. Patrols | Form them if chosen; elect PLs | |
| 7. Good of the Order | Anyone may propose one more house rule; majority vote | |

Item 5 is where the game bends to the table. A family with a six-year-old checks Campfire, no Weather, Crew mode. A Summit table at a merit badge day checks Summit, two Twists, patrols, a 40-minute limit. Same cards, very different afternoon, and the players chose it themselves.

In a campaign the PLC recurs between sessions, shorter: items 4 and 5 only, plus re-election every third session. The PLC sheet becomes the campaign's record of what the table decided, which settles arguments later.

### Individual quest, group effort

The pieces line up like this, so the design keeps both halves honest:

| Yours alone | The table's together | Who runs it |
|---|---|---|
| Sash, ranks, Oath, Individual Quests, EARNED stamps | Group Quests, Crew mode, the Adventure's Reward when shared, Twists, the campaign | SPL (procedure), Scoutmaster (advice and the Trail) |

A player can win a game without ever touching a Group Quest. A table cannot finish a Service Project, survive a Trek in Crew mode or get through a Scouting Year without one.

## 15. Game Two: The PLC (a planning game with real output)

The opening PLC in section 14 sets up a game. This section makes the PLC itself a game, one a troop can run at its real Patrol Leaders' Council to plan a real troop meeting, outing, court of honor or year. The output is not points. It is a filled plan sheet the SPL hands to the Scoutmaster.

Why cards help a real PLC: a quiet Scout can play a card instead of speaking up. A table full of ideas on cards is easier to vote on than ideas in the air. A plan that still has empty slots or constraints with no owner is visibly unfinished. And a 12-year-old PL who has played the Trail game already knows every badge, ceremony and Adventure by sight.

### Which cards are playable

Only the cards that help a plan. Everything else stays in the box, so the PLC game is a strict subset.

| From the Trail game | Used in the PLC game as |
|---|---|
| Badge cards (142) | activity and skills-instruction ideas: "Skills Instruction: First Aid" is the First Aid card in that slot |
| Adventure cards | outing and month ideas: the annual plan is twelve Adventure cards on a calendar |
| Event and Ceremony cards | program items: Flag ceremony, Crossover, Court of Honor, Scout Sunday |
| Good Turn cards | service project ideas |
| Heritage and Scout Law cards | Scoutmaster's Minute topics and a monthly theme |
| Rank cards | advancement planning: which rank requirements this meeting or outing serves |
| Gear cards | the equipment list |
| Helper cards | who to invite: a counselor, a guest, camp staff |

Not playable: Legacy, Quests, Oath mats, Weather, Twists, Ribbons. (Weather and Twists come back in Practice mode, below.)

### New cards for the PLC game

| Card | Count | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Task** | ~10 | the job for this meeting. Each has a complexity level, the plan sheet it produces, the Slots it needs, the Constraints it triggers, and a time budget |
| **Slot** | ~30 | a time block. Troop meeting: Pre-opening, Opening, Skills Instruction, Patrol Time, Game, Closing, Scoutmaster's Minute. Outing: Departure, Arrival and Camp Setup, Saturday Morning Program, Lunch, Afternoon Program, Dinner, Campfire, Cracker Barrel, Scout's Own, Breakdown, Return. Court of Honor: Welcome, Colors, Awards by rank, Merit Badges, Special Awards, Scoutmaster's Minute, Refreshments |
| **Position** | 18 youth + 16 adult | the same Position cards as section 16, used here as the duty roster. A name goes on the card (dry-erase sleeve or sticky note). Adult Positions take the adult-owned Constraints |
| **Constraint** | ~24 | reality checks. Youth-owned: Budget and cost per Scout, Transportation and drivers, Equipment check, Food and dietary needs, Reservation lead time, Permission slips, Weather season and backup plan, Promotion and sign-up deadline, Buddy system, Leave No Trace. Adult-owned (the Scoutmaster signs these): Two-deep leadership, Youth Protection, Medical forms, Activity approval and insurance, Age-appropriate activity check |
| **Plan sheet** | 5 | the deliverable: Troop Meeting Plan, Outing Plan, Court of Honor Program, Service Project Plan, Annual Calendar. The Scribe copies the laid-out cards onto the sheet at the end |

### The Tasks, simple to complex

| Level | Task | Slots | Constraints | Time | Output |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Troop Meeting Agenda** | 7 | 2 (Equipment, Instructor assigned) | 15 min | Troop Meeting Plan |
| 1 | **Patrol Meeting / Patrol Activity** | 4 | 2 | 10 min | Troop Meeting Plan (patrol section) |
| 2 | **Field Trip** | 6 | 5 (Transport, Cost, Permission slips, Two-deep, Food) | 20 min | Outing Plan |
| 2 | **Service Project** | 6 | 5 | 20 min | Service Project Plan |
| 3 | **Weekend Campout** | 11 | 8, plus a full duty roster | 30 min | Outing Plan |
| 3 | **Court of Honor** | 7 | 4 (Awards list from advancement records, Invitations, Venue, Refreshments) | 25 min | Court of Honor Program |
| 4 | **Summer Camp Prep** | 8 | 10, long lead times (Reservation, Medical forms, Merit badge sign-ups) | 40 min | Outing Plan |
| 4 | **High Adventure** | 10 | 12 (adds Fitness and age requirements, Crew Leader, Training) | 45 min | Outing Plan |
| 5 | **Annual Plan** | 12 (one per month) | 6 (Council and district calendar, School calendar, Budget, Summer camp reservation, Fundraising, Court of Honor dates) | 60 to 90 min | Annual Calendar; each month becomes a Level 1 to 4 Task later |

**Choosing the Task.** Three ways, decided by whoever is running the meeting:

- **Directed:** the Scoutmaster or SPL picks the Task the troop actually needs this month. This is the real-PLC mode.
- **Random:** draw a Task card from those at or below a chosen level. For training, so Scouts practice planning things the troop is not about to do.
- **Ladder:** a new PLC starts at Level 1 and moves up one level per meeting until it has planned an Annual Plan. A leadership training course in a box.

### How a PLC round runs

The SPL runs it. The Scoutmaster sits in the back, speaks at step 6, and signs at step 7.

1. **Call to order.** The SPL reveals the Task card and reads its level, output, and time budget. The Scribe takes the matching Plan sheet. Start a timer.
2. **Lay the skeleton.** The SPL places the Slot cards the Task names in order down the middle of the table. Empty slots are the visible to-do list.
3. **Brainstorm by card.** Each Patrol Leader draws a hand of 7 from the playable deck (Badges, Events, Adventures, Good Turns, Heritage), filtered to the Task if the Task says so (a campout deals Outdoors and Nature heavy; a court of honor deals Events and Heritage). Going around the table, each PL plays one card into an empty Slot and says one sentence about it. A PL may pass. Two cards in one Slot means a vote; the loser goes back to the pile. Keep going until every Slot has a card or the table agrees a Slot is "patrol's choice".
4. **Advancement check.** The SPL places the Rank cards the plan serves next to the Slots they serve. A plan that serves no rank gets one Slot changed. This is the step real PLCs skip, and the cards make it hard to skip.
5. **Reality check.** The SPL flips the Task's Constraint cards one at a time. Each one is either resolved on the spot ("Cost: $12 per Scout") and written on the sheet, or assigned an owner by placing it on a Position card. A Constraint with no owner and no answer is an open item, and the plan is not done while any youth-owned Constraint is open.
6. **Scoutmaster's review.** The adult-owned Constraints are turned face up and the Scoutmaster speaks for the first time: yes, or what has to change. Safety is the one place the adult decides.
7. **Duty roster and sign-off.** Names go on the Position cards. The Scribe finishes the sheet from the laid-out cards. The SPL and Scoutmaster sign. Photograph the table before clearing it; the photo is the backup record.
8. **Good of the Order.** One minute. Then the Scoutmaster's Minute if there is time, using the Heritage or Law card the table picked.

A Level 1 Troop Meeting Agenda runs in fifteen minutes with practice. The Annual Plan is an evening, usually at a PLC retreat, and uses the calendar in section 13 as its starting layout.

### Practice mode (training) and the bridge to the Trail game

For Introduction to Leadership Skills or a PLC that is learning, add two things:

- **Curveballs.** Shuffle 3 Weather or Twist cards into the Constraint pile. "Driver cancels Friday", "Rain all weekend", "Camp is full". The table has to replan on the spot. Removed for real planning.
- **Plan rating.** After sign-off, count: every Slot filled (1 each), every Constraint owned (1 each), advancement served (2), within time (2), variety (1 per Trail represented, max 4). Not a score to win, a number to beat next month.

The bridge: a real PLC that finishes a plan earns each Scout at the table a Scout Spirit credit in the current campaign and one Legacy draw for the SPL. A troop that plans its year with the cards walks into its Scouting Year campaign (section 12) with the same twelve Adventures on both calendars.

### What makes it a game and not a worksheet

Turns, a hand, a vote, a timer, empty slots that have to be filled, and a rating to beat. Those are enough. The win condition is a plan the Scoutmaster signs. The thing D&D gets right, that the table builds something together that none of them could have built alone, is the real PLC's job already; the cards just make it visible on the table.

## 16. Position cards: the troop roster

Scouting runs on positions, youth and adult, and every one of them does a job the troop feels when it is missing. The game copies that. Each player holds a **youth** Position and an **adult** Position for the game. Each gives its holder a small perk, but the real effect is on the group: the table's combined picks decide whether the troop eats, has gear, has rides, has two adults, and has records. A troop of buglers wakes up early and goes hungry.

Position cards are the same cards the PLC game uses as its duty roster (section 15). One set, two jobs.

### Troop Needs

Every Adventure lists the Needs it has. A Need is **filled** if at least one revealed Position fills it. Each unfilled Need puts a **Hindrance** on the whole table, including on whoever's duplicate pick left it empty. Most Hindrances have a **Fix** that can happen mid-game. One does not.

| Need | Filled by | Hindrance while unfilled | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Food** | Grubmaster, Patrol Cook (youth) | **Hungry:** every player gains 1 less Effort each turn | Anyone earns Cooking, or the Food Drive Group Quest completes, or a player spends a whole turn as cook |
| **Gear** | Quartermaster (youth); Equipment Coordinator (adult) | **Unpacked:** Gear costs 2 more and Rain Fly does nothing | A player plays 2 Gear cards in one turn |
| **Records** | Scribe, Historian (youth); Advancement Chair, Secretary (adult) | **Lost paperwork:** every requested or yielded Board of Review is scheduled one turn later than it would be (section 17) | A player skips a draw to update the records |
| **Program** | Instructor, Troop Guide, Den Chief, Webmaster, Librarian (youth) | **Boring meeting:** the Adventure's Twist is off, hand size −1 | Anyone earns 2 badges in one turn |
| **Spirit** | Chaplain Aide, Bugler, Cheermaster (youth); Chaplain (adult) | **Grumbling:** no Scout Spirit credits can be earned | Any Good Turn is played |
| **Rides** | Transportation Coordinator, Committee Member (adult) | **Stuck at the church:** the Adventure's Terrain discounts are off | A player spends 3 Effort: a parent drives |
| **Money** | Treasurer, Fundraising Chair (adult) | **Broke:** Events cost 1 more; Summer Camp's free Counselors are off; no trading post | Fundraiser: the table discards 6 cards onto a quest |
| **Two-deep** | the Scoutmaster (always in the troop, below) plus any **one** other adult leader Position revealed: Assistant Scoutmaster, Committee Chair, Committee Member, Chartered Organization Rep | **No outing:** the Adventure's Reward is off and no Challenges can be started | **None.** The only Hindrance with no fix, except the sealed Twist "A parent steps up". The real rule has no workaround either |

Which Needs apply depends on the Adventure, so the same roster is fine for a Troop Meeting (Program, Records) and a disaster for a High Adventure Trek (Food, Gear, Rides, Money, Two-deep). A plain Troop Meeting lists two Needs; a Trek lists six.

### Picking positions: hidden, simultaneous, and collusion-proof

The pick is the fun part. Nobody should know the roster until it is revealed, and nobody should be able to arrange it beforehand. Four safeguards, in order of strength:

1. **You pick from a dealt hand, not from the full set.** Shuffle the youth Positions and deal each player 3; same for the adults, with the Scoutmaster card set aside first, since it is always in the troop and never dealt. You choose one of each from what you were dealt. A pre-arranged "you take Grubmaster, I take Quartermaster" is worthless when neither of you knows what you will hold. This is the safeguard that does the work.
2. **Picks are locked before the Adventure is known.** Face down on the table, then the PLC draws the three Adventures and votes. You cannot tune your pick to the Needs because you do not know them yet.
3. **Simultaneous reveal, no talk.** The SPL counts three and everyone flips. From the deal to the flip, no table talk; the SPL enforces it, and at tables of 6 or more the SPL collects the face-down picks and reveals them all at once so even a glance gives nothing away.
4. **The honor rule, printed on the Oath mat:** "A Scout is Trustworthy. Your pick is your own." It is a Scout game, and it says so.

Two more things make collusion unattractive rather than just forbidden. Duplicates are wasted (the second Bugler fills nothing), so the best roster is the most varied one, and variety is exactly what a secret dealt hand produces on its own. And every Hindrance hits the whole table, including the players who caused it, so there is no "advantage" to arrange: the roster is good or bad for everyone together.

**Reveal moment.** After the flip the SPL reads the Adventure's Needs aloud and the table sees which are filled. A troop with three Buglers and no Grubmaster hears "Hungry" and figures out who is going to earn Cooking. That conversation is the game working.

### Positions in the campaign

- A Position is held for a **term** of three sessions, then the deal and pick happen again. Six months in a troop.
- **Position of Responsibility for rank.** Real Star, Life and Eagle require months in a position. In a campaign, Life requires having held any youth Position for one full term, and Eagle for two. A Scout who swapped every session does not make Eagle, which is the real rule.
- The PLC game uses the same cards as its duty roster, so a troop that has already picked Positions for the campaign walks into planning with the roster filled in.

### Tier

Campfire ignores Positions. Trail uses youth Positions only and the Needs Food, Gear and Program, with adult Needs assumed filled. Summit uses both decks and every Need the Adventure lists.

## 17. Skill cards: show it, don't pay it

In the program, Scout through First Class are earned by skills, not merit badges. The game now does the same. A Skill card is one item from the Scout-to-First-Class requirements, paraphrased, chosen because it can be shown or explained at a table in under a minute with nothing more than what is in the box.

### The props

Three things ship in the box and are the only props any Skill may need:

- **Paracord**, one 4-foot piece per player. Knots, whipping (explained), bowline on yourself.
- **Neckerchief** (or any bandana). Flag folding, triangular bandage, sling, head bandage.
- **Two pencils.** Lashings and fire lays in miniature; a splint.

No knives, no fire, no water, no compass. A Skill that needs any of those is an **Explain** card, never a **Do** card. Fire lays are built from pencils and explained; CPR is explained in steps and never practiced; the pocketknife skill is the safety rules, said aloud.

### Card anatomy

Front, seen by the demonstrator: the skill name, its rank (Scout, Tenderfoot, Second Class, First Class), whether it is **Do** or **Explain**, the prop icon, and the Trail it counts for. Back, read by the checker: the steps or the three key points, and a small diagram for knots and lashings.

### Checking a skill

Whoever checks reads the back of the card. **Do** cards: perform it; the checker compares with the diagram and may ask one question ("show me the working end"). **Explain** cards: say it; the checker ticks that at least two of the three key points were said. Under a minute either way. The checker never says "wrong"; the card says "not yet", and the checker shows the skill. That is the EDGE method (Explain, Demonstrate, Guide, Enable), and it is the only way a Skill is ever taught in the program.

Nothing is ever written on a card. A skill is proven by doing it, in front of the table, every time it matters.

### The Board of Review: how rank is proven

The chain is the real one: **Scoutmaster conference, then Board of Review, then the Advancement Chair records it.** Each step needs a different adult, and the Scoutmaster never sits on the board.

1. **Declare.** At a Board window you declare the rank you want: Tenderfoot, Second Class or First Class. Scout is the floor and needs no test. You may declare any rank, not only the next one up. To declare, you must **hold** at least 3 Skill cards of that rank (collection mode; box mode waives this and tests from the shared Skill deck).
2. **Scoutmaster conference.** Before any board, the Scoutmaster (the adult at the table, or the always-present Scoutmaster card run by the SPL) holds a one-minute conference: name one Skill you hold and say how you would show it, and name one virtue you have lived this game. Nobody fails. "You're ready" sends you to the board; "let's practice one more" means the board waits one turn. The Scoutmaster card holds **one conference per round**, so at a busy table Scouts queue for it. The adult Scoutmaster, when present, holds as many as asked.
3. **The board draws.** The other players are the board. They shuffle your held Skill cards of the declared rank and lower and draw the tests blind: Tenderfoot 1, Second Class 2, First Class 3 (Summit 2, 3, 4). Nobody chooses, so neither you nor a friend can steer the draw. The Scoutmaster and any Assistant Scoutmaster at the table take no part in this step.
4. **Demonstrate.** Each drawn card goes to the player who drew it as checker. If an Advancement Chair, Committee Chair or Committee Member Position holder is at the table they may take any card as checker instead; a committee runs the board when one exists.
5. **Pass all, and it is recorded.** The rank is yours when the **Advancement Chair** records it. With the Advancement Chair Position at the table, flip the Rank card at once. Without one, the rank is passed but not recorded: write it on the Scoutbook sheet, and it takes effect (Rank card flipped, Effort and perks) at the start of the next round if a Scribe or Historian holds the Records Need, otherwise at the next Court of Honor. A passed rank cannot be lost while it waits to be recorded. **Miss any:** "not yet". You are Scout rank until the next Board window, whatever you were before. No card is lost.

**Carry only what you can tie.** The board draws from the cards you hold, so a Skill card you cannot do is a liability in your own binder. Trade it to someone who can. That one rule is what makes sorting the commons worth doing.

### Board windows: sporadic, not easy

- **Opening board**, right after Positions are revealed. Everyone may declare. Always immediate.
- **Court of Honor** Events, two or three in the deck. Anyone may declare when one is played. Always immediate: that is what the event is for.
- **Request**, once per game: give up your whole turn (no draw, no badge) to ask for a board for yourself. Whether it happens now or later is the scheduling rule below.
- **Yielded**: another player gives up their turn to ask for a board for you (Courteous, below). Scheduled the same way.
- **Challenge Up**: the fast lane, below, one rank at a time. Needs no leaders, so it is never delayed.

Between windows your rank is what it is. A First Class Scout who has a bad day at the opening board plays as a Scout, with Scout Effort and no Trek, until the next Court of Honor or until a friend yields a turn. Two or three rounds in practice, never the whole game, and the fastest way back is another player's help, which is the design.

### Scheduling a board: the leaders have to be in the room

A real Board of Review is run by committee members, and a troop waits for one more often than it would like. The game does the same: a requested or yielded board happens **now** only if the table has the leaders for it, and otherwise it is **scheduled**, which costs the Scout something.

**Board capacity.** Each of these adult Positions revealed at the table is one board seat per round: Committee Chair, Committee Member, Advancement Chair, Chartered Organization Representative, Unit Commissioner. A board needs one seat. A seat used this round is used up until the next round. The Scoutmaster and Assistant Scoutmaster are never seats; their part is the conference before the board.

| Request arrives and… | What happens |
|---|---|
| a seat is free | **Immediate.** The board happens now, on the turn the Scout gave up. |
| no seat is free, or the table has none of those Positions | **Scheduled.** "The committee meets Tuesday." The board happens at the start of the Scout's next turn, before anything else. The Scout has already spent this turn asking, and because they are in the board room while the others draw, they **skip their draw** next turn as well. They play the rest of that turn normally once the board is done, at whatever rank the board gave them. |
| the Records Need is unfilled (Lost paperwork, section 16) | **Scheduled one turn later still.** The advancement records are not ready. This replaces the Lost paperwork Hindrance from section 16. |
| a Weather card is on the requesting Scout's camp | **Scheduled.** Nobody holds a board in the rain. A friend's Gear or a Kind cancel clears it, and then the normal rule applies. |

**With a Scoutmaster.** The adult holds the Board calendar and decides, within three bounds printed in the Handbook:

- The Scoutmaster may **find a committee member** so a board happens immediately even with no seat free, once per round, for a Scout who has been at Scout rank for three or more rounds or who has just been Challenged Up and missed. "Let me make a call." The Scoutmaster still does not sit on it; the SPL and another player check. This is the coaching tool, and it is how a bad day stays short.
- The Scoutmaster may **delay** a board that has a free seat, once per player per game, when the request is plainly timed to dodge Weather or beat a Court of Honor. "Not tonight; the committee is at Roundtable." The delay is the standard one: next turn, skip the draw.
- The Scoutmaster may **never** refuse a board outright, delay the same player twice in a game, or delay a yielded board. Someone gave up a turn for that Scout; the kindness is not taken away by an adult.

**Without a Scoutmaster.** The SPL applies the table rule above with no discretion. If the table has no board-seat Positions at all, every requested board is scheduled, which is the real experience of a small troop and a reason to hope someone drew Committee Member.

**What the delay teaches.** The cost is one draw and one round of waiting at a lower rank. It is enough to make a Scout plan a request instead of firing it off, and small enough that the stuck player is never stuck for long. If playtesting shows players refusing to request boards at all, drop the skipped draw and keep only the wait.

### Helping another Scout up

Every one of these lives a virtue, and every one earns the helper a **Leadership** token. Three Leadership tokens are a Scout Spirit credit, and in a campaign a Leadership token counts as a month toward the Position-of-Responsibility terms Life and Eagle require.

| Help | How | Who lives what |
|---|---|---|
| **Coach** | Before the board draws, one player may coach you: they show one of your held Skills, EDGE style, you repeat it. During the board, if you miss a test, the coach may demonstrate it once and you try again. One coach per board. | Coach lives Helpful |
| **Vouch** | A player holding the declared rank or higher vouches for you: the board draws one fewer test (minimum 1). If you miss anyway, the voucher may not vouch again this game. | Voucher lives Loyal |
| **Yield the floor** | A player gives up their turn to open a board for you. | They live Courteous |
| **Challenge Up** | Once per round at the table, a higher-ranked player may challenge a lower-ranked one: they pick (not draw) one Skill card of the challenged player's next rank from the cards that player holds. Pass: the challenged player climbs one rank at once, no board. Miss: nothing happens. The challenger gains nothing but the token. | Challenger lives Kind; the challenged lives Brave |
| **Patrol board** | A patrol declares together. Tests are drawn from the whole patrol's cards and split so each member demonstrates at least one. Pass all: every member takes the rank. Miss: the patrol drops to Scout together. | Patrol Leader lives Loyal |
| **Teach a knot** (Good Turn card) | The target player gets one free retry at their next board. | Helpful, as every Good Turn |
| **Positions** | Instructor and Troop Guide may coach as a free action and may sit as checker on any board. The SPL may call one extra Court of Honor per game. | |

A Scout who never helps anyone up can still make Eagle on badges. A Scout who does gets there sooner and with Leadership to show for it.

### What demonstrating does besides rank

- **Show it, don't pay it.** When you earn a badge, you may demonstrate a Skill of that badge's Trail instead of paying 1 of its Effort. Knots pay for Outdoors badges; first aid pays for Life & Home and Sports & Water; citizenship skills pay for Citizenship & Community. Practice is currency.
- **Teach it.** Any Skill you hold may be taught to another player at any time: you explain and guide, they demonstrate. If you hold a duplicate, they keep the card. The teacher lives Helpful and takes a Leadership token.
- **Skill-off (Summit Challenges).** A Challenge may be a Skill-off instead of a stat comparison: the challenger names a Do Skill, both players perform it at once with their own paracord, first correct one wins the Ribbon. The fastest bowline at the table is now a thing.
- **Campaign.** The Scoutbook sheet records the rank held at each session's Court of Honor Night. Every session opens with a board, so rank is re-proven every time, but a Scout who held a rank at the last Court of Honor faces a **returning board** of one test for it instead of the full count.
- **Campfire (Tier 1).** Show and tell: any child who demonstrates a Do Skill with help plays an extra badge that turn. Knots only, no checker needed; the table claps.
- **PLC game.** Skill cards are the content for the Skills Instruction slot. A troop meeting plan that puts "Bowline" in that slot is placing the card.

## 18. Collecting and trading: the rarity ladder

Section 7 set the top of the ladder: the EARNED foil you get only by finishing a badge at a Bucky event. Skill cards set the bottom. Everything else sits between, and the ladder is the collecting game.

| Tier | Cards | How a Scout gets them | Tradeable |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Common** | Skill (52 kinds, printed in multiples) | starter box, free Skill packs at every event and troop meeting, handed out by Troop Guides, trading | yes, freely |
| **Uncommon** | Badge (142), Gear, Helper, Event, Good Turn | starter box, the full set, print-and-play | yes |
| **Rare** | Adventure, Heritage, Twist | starter box has a few; full set; event prizes | yes |
| **Legacy** | Common Legacy, Uncommon, Rare, Lost | Discovered only (section 11) | yes, and worth the most in trade |
| **Team Member, event edition** | the dated card for that event's counselor (section 19) | one per participant at the event, nothing else | yes; the play edition of the same card is in the set, so trading it moves a keepsake, not power |
| **EARNED foil** | a badge with the foil stamp and date | earned at a Bucky event by finishing that badge | **never** |
| **Structural** | Oath mat, Scout Law, Position, Rank, Quest, Plan sheets | one full set per player in every box | no, and no need to: everyone has them all |

The shape matters: the commonest card is the one a new Scout needs most, and the rarest is the one nobody can buy.

### Skill cards are the commons, on purpose

- **Printed in multiples.** The 52 Skills come in packs of 8, random, weighted toward the lower ranks (4 Scout or Tenderfoot, 3 Second Class, 1 First Class). Packs are free: at every merit badge day, at Test Lab Day, in the print-and-play as a sheet to cut, and as a stack a Troop Guide keeps for new Scouts.
- **Starter deck:** all 10 Scout Skills and all 14 Tenderfoot Skills, so a new player can reach Tenderfoot without trading. Second Class and First Class Skills come from packs and trades, which is where the collecting starts. Full contents below.

### The Starter deck: one player, about 100 cards

One Starter makes a player ready for any Trail table. Two Starters are a complete two-player game with nothing else needed. Four are a troop. The count lands near 100 because a player's personal set (ranks, Law, Skills) is 44 cards before a single badge, and that set is the part that cannot be shared.

### Holding the cards to claim the rank

To declare Tenderfoot, Second Class or First Class at a Board of Review (section 17) you must hold at least 3 Skill cards of that rank, and the board draws your tests from the cards you hold. No cards, no declaration, even if you could tie every knot in the box. That is the collecting hook. Nothing is ever written on a card: the card is the **opportunity**, and the demonstration, every time, is the **achievement**.

Because the tests come from your own binder, the binder you want is not the biggest one. It is the one where you can do every card in it. Trade away what you cannot do yet, or keep it and learn it before the next board. Either is a good day for the game.

### Two ways to play Skills

- **Box mode** (one set, a sit-down game): Skill cards are shuffled into the shared deck and drawn like anything else; a board tests from the box's Skill deck of the declared rank, and the hold requirement is waived.
- **Collection mode** (events, campaigns, league): each player brings their own Skill cards and plays only those; a board tests from what you brought. This is the mode in which collecting and trading matter, and it is the mode for every Bucky event.

### Trading rules

Trading is a scheduled thing, not a mid-turn thing, so a game does not stall.

| When | What |
|---|---|
| **Trading Post** | a 3-minute window before the PLC and again at the Court of Honor; in a campaign, between sessions |
| **Patrol Meeting** | partners may trade within the patrol during the 30-second phase |
| **Scout show / trading post Twist** | some Adventures and Twists open the post mid-game |

| Trade | Rule |
|---|---|
| Skill for Skill | 1 for 1, always |
| Skill for Badge | 2 Skills for 1 Badge, or whatever both agree |
| Badge for Badge | 1 for 1 |
| Legacy or Heritage | whatever both agree; these are the cards collectors chase |
| **EARNED foil** | **never traded, never sold, never given.** It has your name's date on it and it is your merit badge. A foil in someone else's binder is worth nothing to them and the rulebook says so |
| Anything for a Rank, Oath, Position or Law card | never; those are not collectibles |

Three fairness rules, printed on the Trading Post card: both players say the trade aloud before cards move; a Scout younger than 11 trades with a Troop Guide, Patrol Leader or adult at their elbow; and any trade may be undone before the next game starts if either side asks. A Scout is Trustworthy, and a Scout is also eleven.

### Giving, not trading

Two cards move by giving, which is different from trading and the game rewards it:

- **Teaching.** A Skill taught to another player (section 17) gives them a card of their own if you hold a duplicate. Helpful's living trigger fires and the teacher takes a Leadership token.
- **Crossover gift.** When a new Scout joins, every player at the table may hand them one Skill card. The Troop Guide perk and the Crossover Event both expect it.

### The binder

Every Scout who plays in collection mode ends up with a binder: Skill pages sorted by rank, holding only what they can do, a badge page per Trail, the EARNED foils at the front, Legacy cards at the back. It looks exactly like a handbook crossed with a card collection, because that is what it is. A Scout who brings the binder to a merit badge day leaves with a new foil in it.

## 19. Scoutmaster Bucky Team Member cards

The people who actually counsel at Bucky events become cards. A Team Member card is a **counselor for the badges it names**, across Trails, available to the whole table like any counselor (section 5), plus one ability. It is the only card in the game with a real person's name on it, which is why it is rare and why the rules below start with consent.

### Three kinds

| Kind | Who | Covers | Ability | Rotation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Super Counselor** | the regular team: Tyler, Bob, Mary, Tony, David R., David K. | ten named badges, with "and more" printed, because they cover more | one each, see below | the event card every third month |
| **Guest Counselor** | the less-regular team: Kim, Dan, Andrea, Lauren, Jay, Mike, Jerry, and whoever joins | three named badges | one small ability each | the event card in the other months |

### What a Super Counselor does

- **Counselor for ten badges.** Any player may earn any badge named on the card while it is in play. This is the only way to cover badges from several Trails with one card, and a single Super Counselor can replace most of a Counselor list.
- **Discount.** The player whose camp holds the card earns the named badges for 1 less (minimum 1), like any counselor.
- **Ability.** One per card, tied to the job of counseling rather than to the person, so nobody is a caricature:
  - **Office hours:** once per round, one player may earn a named badge as a free action outside their turn.
  - **Partial:** a player who cannot pay a named badge's full cost may pay half, round up, and finish it next turn. A real partial blue card.
  - **Class of ten:** on a Merit Badge Day Adventure, a named badge is never "full"; both players who picked it earn it.
  - **Prerequisites done:** named badges with Effort 3 cost 2 for everyone.
  - **Early finish:** a player who earns a named badge may go to the gathering area: draw 1.
  
  Each Super Counselor **chooses** their own ability from this list, or proposes one. Their card, their call.

### What a Guest Counselor does

Counselor for three named badges, plus one small ability from a shorter list: those badges cost 1 less for everyone; or the first player to earn one of them each game draws 1; or the card may be moved to another player's camp as a free action once per game ("the counselor moved to another table").

### Mrs. Scoutmaster Bucky: the Registration desk

Not a counselor, and the card is honest about it. She is the person who knows where everyone should be.

- **She knows who to call:** once per round, name one badge; that badge counts as having a counselor this round for one player. She finds you a counselor; she does not counsel.
- **Blue cards:** when a Merit Badge Day Adventure is played, every player's first badge that game is a free action.
- **General inquiries:** any player may ask the table one rules question per game and get the answer before deciding, with no turn spent.
- **Parents:** the Rides Need counts as filled while she is in play. Someone always finds a car.

Rotation puts her in a Super Counselor month, so one in six Super slots is the wild card, and every participant that quarter goes home with Mrs. Scoutmaster Bucky.

### The event edition and the play edition

Every participant at every Scoutmaster Bucky event from **2027** receives that event's Team Member card: the event date printed on it, a foil band like the EARNED stamp, one per Scout. Super Counselor months every third month, Guest Counselor months between. Twelve events, twelve cards, four of them Super.

Two things keep this from becoming attend-to-win:

1. **Same rules text, different finish.** Every Team Member card also exists as a **play edition** in the set and in the print-and-play, identical in everything but the date and the foil. The event edition is a keepsake and a trade piece. Power is never exclusive.
2. **Limits.** One Team Member card per deck in Summit. Two in play at a table in Trail. Never in Campfire.

### Consent, likeness and names

Printed rules for the people on the cards, before any card is made:

- **Written yes from each person** before a name goes on a card, covering the name, the art and the ability, with the right to change their badge list or pull the card at any time. Pulled cards leave the rotation; existing copies stay with the Scouts who have them.
- **First names only**, as the team is known at events.
- **Bucky-style art, not photos.** Each Team Member is drawn in the Bucky style, no pants, tan shirt and neckerchief, with one prop for their badges. The same rule the site already follows for Bucky himself.
- **The badge list is theirs.** The ten (or three) badges come from the counselor roster in the Events folder, confirmed by the person. Nothing is printed they do not actually counsel.
- **Nothing personal on the card.** Abilities describe counseling, never the person. No ages, no towns, no contact information. The QR goes to the event page, not to a person.
- **Opt-out for the wild card too.** Mrs. Scoutmaster Bucky's card exists only if she wants it to.

## 20. Worn badges, and the Sustainable Safari pack

### Wearing badges: one Eagle-required, two electives

A sash full of badges is a score. Three of them are also a kit. From the badges in your sash you **wear** up to three at a time: **one Eagle-required badge and two electives**, laid face up in front of your Oath mat. Only worn badges' **Worn** abilities are live (section 5). Everything else in the sash still counts for rank, sets and quests; it just is not switched on.

- **Choosing.** Wear badges at setup if you start with any (EARNED foils, campaign sash), and change what you wear whenever you earn a new badge, at any Court of Honor, and during a Patrol Meeting. Changing is free. You cannot wear a badge you have not earned.
- **The Eagle slot.** Empty until you earn an Eagle-required badge. A player with none wears two electives and no more. That is the quiet push toward the Eagle-required list.
- **The theme is the skill.** A Worn ability is always the badge's subject turned into a standing advantage. Camping improves your campsite, so Weather hurts less. Cooking improves what your patrol eats, so you gain Effort. Lifesaving protects a buddy. Orienteering finds things in the deck. A player reading the card learns what the badge is about from what it does.
- **Tiers.** Trail and Summit use worn badges. Campfire does not; children just collect.
- **Writing rule for the 142.** A Worn ability is one line, fires in most games, and is worth about half a card a turn, less than a Core virtue. It never stacks with itself and never makes another player's worn ability stop working. Eagle-required badges get slightly stronger Worn lines than electives, since only one can be worn.

## 22. Build order

1. Lock the six Trail assignments for all 142 badges (one spreadsheet column).
2. Assign Effort (1–3) and Hands/Know/Care to each badge. Rule of thumb: Eagle-required = Effort 2–3; hands-on badges high Hands; academic badges high Know; service and people badges high Care.
3. Write the ~40 Helper/Event/Gear/Weather cards, the 7 Rank cards and the 12 Scout Law cards.
4. Lay out the frame once as an SVG template; generate all cards from the existing `merit-badges.yaml` and art with a script, the same way the site builds pages.
5. Print one set at home, playtest Campfire with the youngest Scouts you know and Trail with a patrol. Change costs before changing rules.
6. Publish the print-and-play PDF. Hand out the first "EARNED" foils at the next merit badge day.
7. Write Tier 3 abilities only after Tiers 1 and 2 have been played ten times.
8. First Legacy wave: 12 cards, one or two from each rarity tier, drawn as vintage Bucky. Pull years, successors and rarity from `src/data/merit-badge-history` by script.
9. Adventures and Quests after Tier 2 is stable: start with Weekend Campout, Field Trip and Merit Badge Day plus the Eagle Project Group Quest, since those are the ones a merit badge day can test the same afternoon. Write the Scoutbook sheet last.
10. Good Turn cards go in with Tier 2, since they are the simplest instants and the coin is a good first token. Heritage cards wait for the trivia bank, and every trivia answer gets cross-checked against the history data before printing.
11. The PLC sheet is a one-page form and can be written as soon as Tier 2 is stable; test it by having a Scout run the opening without an adult. The Scoutmaster's Handbook and sealed Twists come with the campaign.
12. The PLC game can be built and tested before the Trail game is finished, because it only needs Badge, Event and Adventure cards plus the new Task, Slot, Position and Constraint cards. Start with the Level 1 Troop Meeting Agenda and test it at a real PLC. If it saves the SPL time, build the rest.
13. Position cards come in with Trail (youth only, three Needs) and expand at Summit. Playtest the dealt-hand pick with a table that tries to collude on purpose; if they can still arrange a roster, shrink the dealt hand from 3 to 2.
14. Skill cards can be built first of everything, because they need no art beyond a knot diagram and they are useful at a troop meeting on their own. Print the first Skill packs for the next merit badge day and hand them out free; the trading starts by itself. Print the ten Scout-rank Skills and a piece of paracord and hand them to a new Scout; if the card teaches the knot, the format works. Check every paraphrase against the current requirements before printing, and never print the requirement text itself.
15. Team Member cards: ask each of the five regulars, the three guests and Cheryl in 2026, in that order, with the ability list and a sample card in hand. Build the 2027 rotation only from the people who said yes, and print the first event edition for the first 2027 event.
16. Worn lines for the 142 badges come after the Trail game has been played ten times, Eagle-required badges first. The Sustainable Safari pack can be mocked up before that from the nine lines in section 20 and shown to Alaina and Bob at the March 2027 logistics meeting; it needs their yes on the name and band before any print.
