PAINTING MERIT BADGE
NOTE TO COUNSELOR
SAFETY NOTE
Primary Safety Concerns:
Painting involves chemicals, tools, and ladders that can cause allergic reactions, chronic health
effects, or injury ranging from minor to life-threatening. Follow the current Scouting America SAFE
Checklist and Guide to Safe Scouting.
Before Painting Activities:
Supervision
The Painting merit badge counselor should know paint chemistry and types, surface preparation,
ladder safety, and safe handling of hazardous materials, consistent with the Guide to Safe Scouting
and SAFE Project Tool Use.
Assessment
● Homes and structures built before 1978 are presumed to contain lead-based paint. Before any
project, Scouts must ask a parent or guardian when the structure was built. If it was built before
1978 or the date is unknown, that surface may not be used for the project.
● Scouts must never attempt to remove, sand, or scrape paint that may contain lead. Lead paint
removal is professional, EPA-certified work only and is outside the scope of this badge.
● Scouting America safety guidelines require a helmet and other safety gear when climbing higher
than shoulder height. Work at that height is best left to a trained adult or professional; scaffolding
and lifts are professional-only equipment and are not required for this badge.
● Reference the SAFE Project Tool Use document to confirm all tool and equipment use is age
appropriate. As a rule, Scouting activities may not include tasks that, by law, youth would not be
allowed to perform in the workplace.
● Obey all restrictions and instructions manufacturers impose on their paints, solvents, and other
products.
Fitness
● Counselors should be aware of and accommodate participants who may be allergic or sensitive
to paint fumes, solvents, or other finishing chemicals.
Equipment and Environment
● Provide a clutter-free, well-ventilated work area (open windows and fans). Never mix solvents or
cleaning products together, as this can produce hazardous gases.


● Keep flammable materials away from heat sources, and keep an appropriate fire extinguisher
(Class A/B/C as needed) on hand.
● Ensure all participants wear proper personal protective equipment: safety glasses or goggles, a
respirator appropriate to the product's safety data sheet (SDS), chemical-resistant gloves, heavy-
duty long-sleeved clothing (canvas or denim), and sturdy work shoes or boots.
● Review the SDS for every paint, stain, or solvent used before the activity begins, and read all
product labels carefully.
● Long hair should be tied back or secured, loose jewelry removed, and loose clothing removed or
secured.
COUNSELOR NOTE
Purpose
Help the Scout understand that paint serves two purposes—protecting a surface and decorating it—
while giving them hands-on experience with color theory, paint chemistry, surface preparation, and
application technique. Just as important, help them build an attitude of safety-first planning before
any painting activity begins.
Teaching Tips

● Emphasize surface preparation as the most important (and most tedious) step: cleaning,
sanding with the grain, priming bare wood or metal, and masking correctly (apply tape bottom-
up, peel it back at a 90-degree angle) largely determine whether a paint job holds up.
● Teach color theory hands-on: primary, secondary, and intermediate colors; warm versus cool
colors; color schemes (complementary, analogous, triad); and how natural versus electric light
changes how a color reads in a room.
● Compare paint types side by side—oil-based versus water-based (latex/acrylic), alkyd versus
polyurethane—and sheen levels from flat to gloss, matching type and sheen to the surface's real-
world use.
● Demonstrate brush and roller technique: matching bristle type (synthetic for latex, natural
bristle for oil or varnish) and roller nap length to the surface, using short overlapping strokes, and
working while edges are still wet.
● Have Scouts test paints and stains on scraps first, and practice calculating paint quantity and
area coverage before starting so they don't run out mid-project.


● Reinforce cleanup and disposal: proper tool cleaning and storage, and disposing of solvents
and paint per local, state, and federal regulations.
